Laminate Timber Flooring Explained: Cost, Durability and Best Use Cases

Ask Oz Timber FloorMore guides

Laminate flooring is one of those options people often shortlist when they want the look of timber without the higher price tag. It can make a room feel cleaner, newer and more finished, especially when the old flooring is scratched, dated or hard to maintain.

For many homes, rental properties and office spaces, laminate timber flooring in Sydney can be a practical choice. It gives you a timber-style finish, comes in a wide range of colours and is usually more budget-friendly than solid timber or engineered timber.

The main thing is knowing where laminate works well and where it may not be the right choice.

What Is Laminate Timber Flooring?

Laminate timber flooring is a multi-layered flooring product designed to look like real timber. It is not made from solid wood. Instead, it usually has a core board, a printed timber-look design layer and a protective wear layer on top.

That top layer helps protect the floor from everyday scratches, marks and stains. The printed design gives the floor its timber appearance, with options that can look like oak, blackbutt, spotted gum, walnut and other popular timber styles.

People often choose laminate because it offers:

  • A timber-look finish at a lower cost 
  • Many colour and style choices 
  • Easy cleaning 
  • Good scratch resistance for daily use 
  • A faster installation process 
  • A practical option for rental and investment properties 

If you are comparing timber flooring options in Sydney, laminate is best seen as a smart visual alternative rather than a real timber product.

Laminate Flooring vs Real Timber Flooring

The biggest difference is material. Real timber flooring uses genuine hardwood or a real timber veneer. Laminate uses a photographic timber-look layer.

That does not make laminate a bad choice. It simply means it plays a different role.

Real timber has natural grain, texture and long-term refinishing potential. It can add strong character to a home. 

Laminate is more cost-effective, easier to clean and often suits projects where budget and speed matter more than a fully natural timber surface.

If you want a premium timber feel, you may prefer engineered timber flooring or solid timber. If you want affordable laminate flooring in Sydney for a practical upgrade, laminate may make more sense.

Pros and Cons of Laminate Flooring

Like every flooring option, laminate has strengths and limits.

Pros:

  • More affordable than many timber flooring options 
  • Wide range of styles and colours 
  • Easy to clean with regular sweeping and light mopping 
  • Good resistance to scratches and everyday wear 
  • Suitable for many bedrooms, living rooms and rental spaces 
  • Faster to install than many traditional flooring types 

Cons:

  • Not real timber 
  • Cannot be sanded and refinished like solid timber 
  • May feel harder or less natural underfoot 
  • Can be affected by water if moisture enters the joins 
  • Needs a clean and level base before installation 
  • Not always suited to wet areas 

Good laminate can look very polished, but it still needs the right product and installation. Poor preparation can lead to movement, gaps or a noisy floor underfoot.

That is why laminate timber flooring installation in Sydney should always start with a proper check of the existing surface.

Where Laminate Flooring Works Best

Laminate flooring works well in dry internal areas where you want a neat timber-look finish without spending as much as real timber.

It can suit:

  • Bedrooms 
  • Living rooms 
  • Hallways 
  • Home offices 
  • Rental properties 
  • Apartments 
  • Investment properties 
  • Office spaces 
  • Retail areas with suitable traffic levels 

For residential laminate flooring in Sydney, it is often chosen for family homes, units and renovation projects where style, cost and simple maintenance all matter.

The product should be selected carefully. Offices, showrooms and some retail spaces may suit laminate, but heavy traffic areas need the right wear rating and professional installation.

Where Laminate Flooring Is Not Recommended

Laminate is not the best choice for every room.

You should be careful using laminate in:

  • Bathrooms 
  • Laundries 
  • Wet rooms 
  • Outdoor areas 
  • Spaces with regular standing water 
  • Areas with serious moisture issues 
  • Uneven subfloors that have not been repaired 

Some products are marketed as waterproof laminate flooring in Sydney, but it is still important to check the product details. Water resistance can vary a lot between brands. Even moisture-resistant laminate needs careful installation and proper care around spills.

If your space has moisture risks, hybrid flooring may be a better option because many hybrid products are designed with stronger water resistance.

Laminate vs Engineered Timber Flooring

Laminate and engineered timber can look similar from a distance, but they are very different products.

Engineered timber has a real timber surface. Laminate has a printed timber-look layer. This means engineered timber feels closer to natural timber and may add a more premium finish to a home.

Laminate is usually more affordable and easier to maintain. Engineered timber can offer better natural warmth and a more refined look.

Choose laminate if your main goals are cost, easy cleaning and a timber-look finish.

Choose engineered timber if you want real timber character with better stability than solid hardwood.

A flooring supplier can help you compare samples in person, since colour, texture and finish often look different once they are inside your home.

Laminate vs Hybrid Flooring

Hybrid flooring and laminate are both popular for people who want practical timber-look floors.

The main difference is water resistance. Hybrid flooring usually performs better in moisture-prone areas, which is why many homeowners choose it for kitchens, dining areas and busy family spaces.

Laminate can still be a good choice for dry rooms, especially if cost is a key factor. Hybrid may cost more, but it can offer extra durability and water resistance for active households.

How Much Does Laminate Flooring Cost in Australia?

Laminate flooring costs vary depending on the product, thickness, wear rating, underlay, room size and subfloor condition.

As a working guide in Australia:

  • Laminate materials may cost around $20 to $80 per m² 
  • Professional installation may cost around $25 to $40 per m² 
  • Fully installed laminate flooring may sit around $55 to $110 per m² 

Your final quote may change if the old flooring needs to be removed, the subfloor needs repair, or floor levelling is required before installation.

This is why it helps to get a site-specific quote rather than relying only on online price ranges.

FAQs

What is laminate timber flooring?

Laminate timber flooring is a layered flooring product designed to look like timber. It has a printed timber-look surface and a protective wear layer, but it is not made from solid wood.

Is laminate flooring durable?

Yes, laminate can be durable for bedrooms, living rooms, hallways, apartments and some commercial spaces. The durability depends on the product quality, wear rating, installation and daily use.

Is laminate flooring waterproof?

Most laminate flooring is not fully waterproof. Some products offer better water resistance, but standing water and moisture around the joins can still cause problems. Hybrid flooring may be better for areas with higher moisture exposure.

How long does laminate flooring last?

Good laminate flooring can last for many years when installed properly and maintained well. Lifespan depends on the product quality, traffic level, cleaning routine and whether the subfloor was prepared correctly.

Is laminate cheaper than timber flooring?

Yes, laminate is usually cheaper than solid timber and engineered timber. It is often chosen by homeowners, renovators and property investors who want a timber-look floor at a lower price.
